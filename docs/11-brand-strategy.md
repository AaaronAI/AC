# 11 · Brand Strategy — Positioning, Voice, Naming

Owner: Brand. Conforms to `DECISIONS.md` (D-002, D-016, D-020). Visual execution of
this strategy lives in `12-design-system.md`.

## 1. Positioning

**Category:** the marketplace for real-world sponsorship — people, moments, objects,
spaces, events, and stunts.

**One-line positioning:** *SponsorThis makes anything sponsorable — and makes
sponsoring it as clear, safe, and provable as buying an ad.*

**Personality (in tension, on purpose):**

| We are | Which means | We are not |
|---|---|---|
| Bold | Big type, direct claims we can keep, no hedging filler | Loud for its own sake |
| Playful, slightly weird | We find a poker table or a dog-walking day genuinely delightful ad space | Jokey, meme-chasing, unserious about money |
| Highly credible | Contracts, proof, state machines, visible fees — the boring parts done beautifully | Corporate, beige, jargon-filled |
| Transparent | Pricing on every page; disclosure celebrated, never hidden | "Contact us for pricing" |
| Human & editorial | Written like a sharp magazine, photographed like real life | Stock-photo gloss, gig-economy cheapness |
| Premium-not-corporate | Confident restraint; one vivid color; whitespace | Luxury pastiche or startup-gradient soup |

**Two failure modes we actively design against:**

1. **Gig-economy cheapness** — we never talk about sellers as "gigs," "hustles," or
   "side income machines." Sellers are people with moments worth sponsoring; the
   money language is "payout," "campaign," "deliverables," never "earnings potential."
2. **Hidden-advertising vibes** — nothing we say may imply covert placement. The
   product is *proudly visible* sponsorship (see §6). "Stealth," "seamless,"
   "organic-looking," and "blends right in" are banned words in marketing copy.

## 2. Brand story

Every surface in the world already carries attention: the regular at the poker
tournament, the dog walker with a route through three parks, a hundred bike commutes
across Denver. None of it is for sale — not because nobody wants it, but because
there's no way to buy it: no price, no contract, no proof, no one to call.

SponsorThis builds that missing machinery. We find real people and real moments, put
honest prices and clear deliverables on them, handle approvals and payments, and
deliver timestamped proof that it happened. Brands get the one thing digital ads can't
fake — genuine presence in the real world. People get paid, fairly and transparently,
for moments that were always valuable.

We start small on purpose: one city (Denver), a curated catalog, a human running every
campaign. The story we tell is the story that's true — a managed marketplace earning
its way to scale one completed, provable campaign at a time (D-001, D-020).

## 3. Messaging hierarchy

| Level | Copy | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Tagline | **Make anything sponsorable.** | Logo lockup, footer, one-liners (D-002) |
| Hero | **Sponsor anything. Show up anywhere.** | Homepage H1 |
| Hero sub | Book real people, real moments, and real-world activations — with clear deliverables and proof. | Homepage subhead |
| Alternative hero | The world is full of ad space nobody can buy. Until now. | Campaign/press/alt landing variant |
| Secondary line | Real people. Real moments. Real-world attention. | Section headers, social bios (D-002) |
| Primary CTA (buyer) | **Find something to sponsor** | Header + hero |
| Primary CTA (seller) | **List something sponsorable** | Header + hero |
| Proof pillar | Every campaign ends with timestamped proof — or your money back. *(Only once refund policy copy is counsel-reviewed; until then: "Every campaign ends with timestamped proof.")* | Trust sections |
| Transparency pillar | Prices on every listing. Fees on every receipt. | Pricing page |
| Disclosure pillar | Proudly sponsored. Every placement is disclosed — that's the point. | Trust & safety, listing pages |

Message priority when space is scarce: (1) what it is (sponsor real things), (2) that
it's safe and provable, (3) that pricing is visible, (4) that it's delightful/weird.
Never lead with weird alone — weird without credible reads as a prank.

## 4. Voice & tone rules

Voice: a sharp, warm editor who takes the money seriously and the world lightly.
Rules: short sentences; verbs over adjectives; numbers stated exactly; jokes allowed
in headlines, never in payment/legal/dispute copy; no exclamation marks in
transactional messages; no fabricated specifics ever (D-020).

Do/don't examples:

| # | Context | ✅ Do | ❌ Don't | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Homepage feature blurb | "Sponsor a regular at a $200 Denver poker tournament. Your logo at the table, photos delivered, venue-approved." | "Dominate the felt! Get MASSIVE exposure with our poker influencer network!" | Specific and honest beats hype; there is no "network" |
| 2 | Seller recruitment | "You already do something worth sponsoring. We handle pricing, contracts, and getting you paid — you keep 80%." | "Turn your life into a passive income stream with the gig economy's hottest new hustle!" | Anti-gig-cheapness rule; states the real D-005 split |
| 3 | Audience claims | "Est. 120 attendees per tournament (venue's stated capacity — seller-provided, not verified)." | "Reach thousands of engaged consumers!" | D-020: evidence field or no claim |
| 4 | Payment status | "Payment authorized. We only capture it when your seller confirms — usually within 48 hours." | "Woohoo! 🎉 Your money is on its way to somewhere awesome!" | Money copy is calm, exact, unfunny |
| 5 | Disclosure framing | "Proudly sponsored. Every placement carries clear disclosure — audiences trust what doesn't hide." | "Blends seamlessly into authentic content — audiences won't even notice it's an ad." | Hidden-advertising vibe is a brand-killer and an FTC problem (A-14) |
| 6 | Empty browse state | "The catalog is small and hand-picked — 25 Denver listings and growing. Post a brief and we'll source options for you." | "Thousands of amazing opportunities are waiting!" | Honest scarcity is a premium signal (D-001, D-020) |
| 7 | Dispute email | "We've received your dispute. A human will review both sides within 3 business days. Your payout release is paused until then." | "Uh-oh! Looks like something went wrong! Don't worry, we'll sort it out! 😅" | Serious moments get serious tone |
| 8 | Stunt marketing | "We sponsored 100 bike commutes. Here's exactly what it cost and what the brand got." | "This growth hack broke the internet." | Editorial show-the-receipts posture |

### Language guardrails (quick reference)

| Banned in marketing copy | Say instead |
|---|---|
| "gig," "hustle," "passive income" | "campaign," "payout," "sponsorship" |
| "stealth," "seamless," "organic-looking," "blends in" | "proudly sponsored," "clearly disclosed" |
| "guaranteed impressions/reach" | the actual deliverables, with evidence labels |
| "escrow" | "payment held until proof is accepted" (D-013 legal language rule) |
| "thousands of listings/opportunities" | the real count ("25 hand-picked Denver listings") |

## 5. Naming evaluation

Scoring 1–5 (5 best). **Trademark-risk scores are reasoned guesses from general
knowledge only — no trademark database, USPTO search, or domain registrar was
queried; real clearance requires counsel and is logged in
`LEGAL-REVIEW-QUESTIONS.md`. Specifically: sponsorthis.com availability could not be
verified from this environment.** ".com plausibility" = how plausibly an exact or
acceptable-variant .com could be obtained at reasonable cost; it is a judgment, not a
lookup.

| Name | Memorability | Category fit | Verb-ability | Trademark risk (5 = low) | Spelling / pronunciation | .com plausibility | Total /30 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **SponsorThis** | 4 | 5 | 5 — "just SponsorThis it"; CTA doubles as name | 3 — "sponsor" is a crowded term; compound may be clearable; needs counsel | 5 | 3 — two-word compounds are often taken/parked; unverified | **25** |
| Patron | 4 | 3 — art-patronage tone, misses stunts/objects | 2 | 1 — collides with well-known marks incl. a famous spirits brand and a major creator platform's naming space | 5 | 1 | 16 |
| Adjacent | 4 | 2 — clever but explains nothing | 1 | 3 | 4 | 2 | 16 |
| Placemark | 3 | 3 — spatial, misses people/moments | 2 | 2 — mapping-software connotations and likely prior marks | 4 | 2 | 16 |
| BackedBy | 3 | 4 — sponsorship-native ("backed by Acme") | 3 | 3 | 3 — spoken ambiguity ("backed by what?") | 3 | 19 |
| Signpost | 4 | 3 — signage metaphor, generic word | 2 | 2 — common English word, crowded | 5 | 1 | 17 |
| Sponsorable | 3 | 5 — literally the category | 2 — adjective, not a verb | 3 | 3 — long, easy to typo | 3 | 19 |
| RealEstate-style coinage: "Momnt" | 2 — vowel-dropped coinages age badly | 3 | 2 | 4 | 1 | 4 | 16 |

**Conclusion (matches D-002):** **Keep SponsorThis, provisional.** It is the only
candidate scoring ≥4 on memorability, category fit, and verb-ability simultaneously —
the name literally is the buyer CTA and the share-card button ("Sponsor This," see
`12-design-system.md` §8), which no alternative replicates. Risks to resolve before
spending on brand equity: (a) trademark clearance by counsel, (b) domain acquisition
or an acceptable variant (e.g., sponsorthis.co / getsponsorthis.com) — neither
verifiable from this environment. Fallback shortlist if clearance fails: BackedBy,
Sponsorable. Revisit checkpoint: before any paid brand spend or incorporation of the
name in legal entity naming.

## 6. Audience-facing disclosure principle

Disclosure is not a compliance footnote — **it is the brand.**

- Every sponsorship on SponsorThis is visibly disclosed to its real-world and online
  audience: `#ad`/`#sponsored` on content, visible signage or verbal disclosure at
  physical moments, per campaign checklist (D-016, A-14).
- We market this as a feature: **"Proudly sponsored."** The pitch to brands: audiences
  reward honesty; the pitch to audiences: you will always know, and that's why this
  is fun instead of creepy.
- Product enforcement: disclosure is a required, non-skippable checklist item; proof
  packages must include disclosure evidence before payout (see
  `09-product-requirements.md` §5.4).
- Copy rule: we never sell reach *around* disclosure. Any partner asking to minimize
  disclosure is declined, and the trust-safety page says so.

2. Does "Proudly sponsored" become a visible on-asset mark (a small stamp sellers

3. Secondary line ("Real people. Real moments. Real-world attention.") vs. hero sub —
   both are canonical (D-002 + this doc); confirm the homepage uses hero sub and
   reserves the secondary line for social bios to avoid stacking two slogans.
