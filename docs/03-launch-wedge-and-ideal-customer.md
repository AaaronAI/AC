## 1. Why Denver (D-003)

- **Founder proximity.** The managed motion (D-001) is founder-operated; the founder is
  on the ground in Denver. In month 1–3 this dominates every other criterion.
- **Dense, event-heavy culture.** FACT: ~378k metro small businesses (JPMC Institute,
  doc 01); REPORTED: 450+ annual festivals/events (single-source, doc 01). Outdoor/
  active lifestyle generates exactly our inventory types (runs, climbs, patios, dogs,
  bikes, breweries).
- **Startup + agency base.** FACT/REPORTED: ~325 marketing/ad agencies tracked in the
  area (Clutch, doc 01) — enough for priority-1 outbound without leaving town.
- **Manageable friction.** Mid-size city: permits and venue relationships are navigable
  by one person (A-16 tests this); media market small enough that a good stunt gets
  local press (D-017).
- **What Denver is not:** the biggest market. That's fine — the wedge exists to validate
  A-01 and the ops model, not to maximize year-1 GMV. No second city until Denver clears
  the `20-city-expansion.md` gates (D-018).

## 3. Ideal Customer Profiles (top 3)

### ICP-1 · The activation-hungry agency

- **Firmographics:** 5–50 person marketing/creative/PR agency in Denver metro; has
  consumer or lifestyle clients; sells "ideas," not just media buying.
- **Person:** account director or new-business lead who must bring "something fresh" to
  quarterly client planning.
- **Trigger events:** client brief asking for "local," "authentic," or "experiential on a
  budget"; lost pitch to a flashier agency; client entering the Denver market.
- **Why us:** we are white-label-able arms and legs — packaged, priced, proof-backed —
  letting them sell an activation without staffing one.
- **Disqualifiers:** pure performance-marketing shops (will force the CPM frame — doc 00
  risk #4); agencies wanting >30 days net payment terms; agencies demanding exclusivity
  over our inventory without volume commitment.

### ICP-2 · The content-starved consumer startup

- **Firmographics:** Denver-area consumer/DTC startup, seed–Series A, 2–25 people,
  spending ≥ $2k/mo on social/creator content already.
- **Person:** founder or first marketing hire; owns brand + growth in one job.
- **Trigger events:** launch (product, funding announcement); a competitor's viral
  moment; UGC pipeline running dry; planning a local presence for a seasonal peak.
- **Why us:** one $750–$1,500 package = a real-world story + a week of content, cheaper
  per asset than their current creator spend (doc 01 §3 reasoning).
- **Disqualifiers:** pre-launch/no budget; regulated products before our policy work
  (cannabis, supplements-with-claims); anyone demanding guaranteed impressions (we
  refuse by policy, D-008).

### ICP-3 · The visible-in-the-neighborhood local business

- **Firmographics:** single- or few-location Denver business (gym, brewery, café,
  clinic, home services) with ≥ $500/mo marketing spend, owner-operated.
- **Person:** the owner.
- **Trigger events:** new location opening; slow season; a festival happening on their
  block; annoyance at Yelp/Google ads ROI.
- **Why us:** $450–$1,000 buys them a neighborhood moment (run club, dog day, patio
  event) with photos they can post for months — versus a $300 booth they'd have to staff.
- **Disqualifiers:** businesses at < $250 budget (below D-005 minimum); those wanting
  coupons-redeemed-style attribution guarantees; anyone we'd have to chase for payment
  (prepay only at launch).

## 4. Seller-side ICP (who makes a great early seller)

The managed model means early supply is **curated, not open** (D-001). We recruit ~25–50
listings (A-05) that look like:

- **Reliable and reachable:** responds same-day; has done organized things before (race
  entries, event hosting, gigging) — reliability is a listed value factor (doc 02 §5).
- **Already doing the activity** — we sponsor real behavior, not invented performances;
  authenticity is the inventory.
- **Controls the venue or can get consent fast:** owns the truck/bike/space, or has a
  warm venue relationship (A-06 leverage).
- **Content-capable:** can shoot passable vertical video, or tolerates our contractor
  shooting them (creative labor line, doc 05).
- **18+, disclosure-willing, background-clean for the inventory type** (D-016).
- **Economically satisfied at 80% payout** (A-03): the payout must beat their
  alternative (typically $0 — nobody is buying this inventory today), which is why we
  expect high acceptance; falsifier is >40% declining on fee grounds.
- **Anti-profile:** clout-chasers proposing unsafe stunts; anyone whose listing depends
  on a venue they've never spoken to; sellers wanting to negotiate price per buyer
  (breaks fixed-package model at launch).

## 5. Validation plan — what the interviews must establish

20 buyer + 25 seller interviews (scripts exist; results do not — ASSUMPTIONS.md). The
ranking in §2 is provisional until these thresholds are met. Interviews are evidence;
only paid campaigns are proof (A-01).

**Buyer interviews (20: ≥8 agencies, ≥6 startups/DTC, ≥6 local businesses) must establish:**

1. **Price acceptance:** unprompted reaction to three real package cards at $450 / $750 /
   $1,500 (D-008 anchors). Pass: ≥50% of a segment says a stated price is "reasonable"
   AND ≥3 buyers total attempt to buy or ask for a proposal. (A-01)
2. **Frame check:** do they evaluate us against influencer content, event booths, or CPM
   media? (Determines pitch; doc 00 risk #4. If >60% force the CPM frame, re-rank
   segment 7 down and rewrite positioning.)
3. **Repeat mechanics:** what would trigger a second purchase; who else signs off; budget
   line it comes from (marketing vs. content vs. events). (A-02, A-10)
4. **Fee visibility:** reaction to the 5% buyer fee shown as a line item. (A-04)
5. **Proof value:** rank proof package components (photos, video, geotag, usage rights) —
   what actually drives rebooking intent. (A-10)

**Seller interviews (25) must establish:**

1. **Supply reality:** ≥20 credible, moderation-passable listing concepts surfaced
   (A-05 falsifier: <20 after 25 interviews → wedge inventory assumption fails).
2. **Payout acceptance:** ≤40% decline at 80% share (A-03).
3. **Venue chains:** for venue-dependent concepts, does the seller have an actual path
   to written approval? (A-06 early signal.)
4. **Reliability screen:** responsiveness, history of completing organized commitments.
5. **Time-to-live:** can the listing be packaged (photos, deliverables, price) in ≤1 week?

**Re-ranking rules (pre-committed, so we can't rationalize later):**
- Agency repeat intent < startup repeat intent at interview stage → flag A-02, keep D-004
  order for 30 days, re-rank at n≥10 paid campaigns.
- <3 of 20 buyers show purchase intent → A-01 in jeopardy; invoke doc 00 kill-criteria
  review before spending on CAC.
- Local businesses close fast but 100% one-shot → keep them for case studies, cap at
  ~30% of founder selling time.
