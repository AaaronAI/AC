# 20 · City Expansion

Governing decision: D-018 (PROVISIONAL) — Denver → Austin → Miami → New York → Los
Angeles → Chicago → Las Vegas. This doc defines the gates no city opens without, challenges
the ordering with what we could verify, and records the repeatable launch playbook.
D-003 stands: no second city until Denver clears these criteria.

---

## 1. City-launch criteria (ALL must be true before a city opens)

| # | Criterion | Gate |

Scorecard lives in the ops workbook; reviewed monthly; a city may be **prepped** (weeks -6
to 0 below) only after C6 is met in the prior city.

## 2. Challenging the D-018 ordering — what we verified, what we assume

Verified external facts (permit friction proxies; fetched 2026-08-28):

- **Austin:** centralized Austin Center for Events; preliminary decision within 10 business
  days; application processing fees ~$211–$316 by tier (FY 2025–26 schedule); Tier 1 events
  fee-free. Sources: [ACE permitting process](https://www.austintexas.gov/ace-event-planning-guide/special-event-permitting-process),
  [FY26 fee schedule](https://www.austintexas.gov/sites/default/files/files/ACE/ACE_Fee_Schedule_2026.pdf).
  → Low-to-moderate friction, fast answers.
- **Miami (Miami Beach):** special-event application fee $333 + permit fee $333, security
  deposits $2,500–$20,000, square-footage fees, and City Commission approval required
  during "High Impact Periods" (e.g., Art Week). Source:
  [Miami Beach special events](https://www.miamibeachfl.gov/city-hall/tourism-culture/special-events/frequently-asked-questions/).
  → High friction and high cost for anything on sand/right-of-way; but a dense brand-
  activation calendar (Art Basel/Miami Art Week) that buys exactly what we sell.
- **New York:** SAPO street-activity application fee $25 (plus real costs downstream);
  film permits via MOME with a $300 application fee. Sources:
  [SAPO](https://nyc-business.nyc.gov/nycbusiness/description/street-activity-permit),
  [MOME fee info](https://home.nyc.gov/site/mome/permits/when-permit-required.page).
  → Cheap paper, heavy process; commercial/promotional street events get real scrutiny.
- **Los Angeles:** FilmLA still-photo permit $104; full motion permit $931; fees held flat
  for FY 2026/27. Sources: [FilmLA fees](https://info.filmla.com/filming-related-fees/common-fees-la-city),
  [FY26/27 fee notice](https://filmla.com/client-jurisdiction-fee-increases-effective-july-1-2026/).
  → Well-trodden, priced, predictable — friction is cost, not uncertainty.
- **Chicago:** DCASE special-event applications accepted up to 180 days out, minimum 21
  days before the event. Source: [chicago.gov special events](https://www.chicago.gov/city/en/depts/dca/supp_info/neighborhood_festivals.html).
  → Moderate friction; hard winter seasonality is the bigger issue (unverified impact, but
  a Nov–Mar launch there fights weather for outdoor inventory).
- **Las Vegas (Clark County):** special-event packages accepted up to 13 months but no less
  than 1 month out. Source: [Clark County special events](https://www.clarkcountynv.gov/government/departments/public_works_department/programs_and_services/special-events).
  → County process is manageable; the real constraint is that the highest-value inventory
  sits on **private casino property** with strict brand/sponsorship control and exclusive
  partnership deals — that, not permits, is why D-018 puts it last (see §3).

**Labeled hypotheses (not verified from this environment):** agency density ranks roughly
NYC > Chicago ≈ LA > Miami ≈ Austin ≈ Denver among these cities (holding-company HQ
concentration in NYC/Chicago); Austin has the strongest startup/DTC-per-capita presence of
the non-coastal group plus a marquee event calendar (SXSW, F1 weekend, ACL); Miami's
event calendar (Art Week, Miami Music Week, boat show) is the most activation-buyer-dense
per week of any candidate. Treat these as directional until we pull firm counts (agency
directories, Crunchbase DTC filters) during city-prep.

### Honest challenge of the ordering

- **Austin second: agree.** Closest culturally to the Denver playbook (weird-friendly,
  outdoor, startup-dense), verified fast permitting, and drivable-ish ops distance. Risk:
  smaller agency market (hypothesis) — demand may lean DTC/local, which is our segment 2–3,
  not 1 (D-004). Acceptable: city #2 tests replication, not segment mix.
- **Miami third: challenge.** Verified permit cost/friction is the highest on the list, and
  buyers concentrate around event windows — a mistimed launch strands supply. Miami is
  excellent **if and only if** launch is timed to land supply and pipeline ~8 weeks before
  Art Week (permit deadlines for Art Week activations fall in late August per local permit
  guides). Otherwise NYC's year-round agency demand (our #1 segment) is the stronger third.
- **NYC fourth: mildly challenge upward.** Largest agency market (hypothesis) = largest pool
  of D-004 segment-1 repeat buyers; SAPO paper cost is trivial; process weight is real but
  our inventory (people, private venues, ordinary-use moments) mostly avoids street-event
  permits. Counterargument for keeping it fourth: ops cost, noise, and the founder can't
  personally run it — NYC deserves a proven playbook and a strong local operator, not a
  v1.1 playbook.
- **LA fifth, Chicago sixth: agree, weakly held.** LA friction is predictable (FilmLA);
  Chicago is fine but winter-gated — whichever slot lands in Q2–Q3 should take Chicago.
- **Las Vegas last: agree, and record why (D-018):** the city's flagship venues are private
  casino resorts with tight sponsorship/brand exclusivity control; casino-adjacent inventory
  (like poker) faces the heaviest venue-approval walls anywhere, and off-Strip inventory
  loses most of the city's attention value. Vegas should open only when we have a national
  buyer base that books around conventions/fights — i.e., demand that pre-exists the city.

### Recommendation

Keep **Denver → Austin** fixed. Make slot 3 **conditional: Miami if the calendar hits
(supply + pipeline ready ~8 weeks before Miami Art Week), otherwise NYC third and Miami
fourth, timed to the next Art Week.** LA fifth, Chicago sixth (swap those two freely on
operator availability/season), **Las Vegas seventh always.** This preserves D-018's intent
(it stays PROVISIONAL); proposing the conditional swap to the decision log when Denver
hits C6 — DECISIONS.md changes first per its own header before any doc builds on the swap.

## 3. The 6-week city-launch playbook (weeks −6 → 0)

| Week | Supply | Demand | Ops |
|---|---|---|---|
| −6 | Local operator hired (contractor, doc 07-trained); 25 seller-interview targets listed from local clubs/organizers/venues | Import national/remote buyers wanting this city (waitlist from doc 17 §5); 100-prospect local list built per D-004 mix | Permit playbook drafted (city equivalents of doc 19 notes); insurance check |
| −5 | 10 seller interviews; 8 candidate listings | Founder outbound opens (doc 18 sequences, city-localized); anchor-buyer target: 2 agencies + 2 DTC | 5 venue-partner conversations started |
| −4 | 15 more interviews; 15 listings drafted | 10 discovery calls; first-stunt buyer shortlist | First stunt selected from doc 19 table, localized; permits filed if needed (mind lead times: park-style permits can need 60+ days — file earliest) |
| −3 | 20 listings; moderation pass (D-016) | 3–5 proposals out (48h SLA holds) | Content contractor booked (C7); pre-cleared venue list ≥ 3 |
| −2 | **25 listings = C1 met** | **20 qualified in pipeline = C2 met**; first paid campaign closed (payment terms per doc 18 §10) | Dry-run of doc 07 runbook with operator; backup-seller bench built |
| −1 | Bench: +5 backup sellers | 2nd–3rd paid campaigns closed | Launch stunt final logistics; local press list (real story only) |
| 0 | — | — | **Launch = execute first stunt as a paid campaign**; proof within 48h; case study within 72h of completion; city scorecard reviewed weekly thereafter |

Budget per city launch (estimate, unvalidated): $4–8k — operator ~$2–4k for the prep window,
stunt hard costs $0.5–1.5k, travel $1–2k, permits/insurance $0.5k. Target: first-month city
revenue ≥ launch cost (mirrors D-010's cash-first posture; set the exact city-level goal
when Denver data exists).
