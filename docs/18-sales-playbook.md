# 18 · Sales Playbook

Ready-to-use kit for founder-led sales. Honesty rules apply to every word (D-020): we are a
**new Denver marketplace** with a founder story and zero fabricated traction. Never imply
existing clients, results, or press we don't have. `{braces}` = personalization slots you MUST
fill; sending an unfilled slot is a fireable offense in a one-person company.

Pricing you may quote: packages from $450 (poker seed listing tiers $450/$750/$1,500 per
D-008; buyer pays +5% service fee per D-005; $250 platform minimum), managed campaigns
from $1,500 (D-006). Target range $750–$5,000.

---

## 1. Cold email sequences

Rules: plain text, no images, one CTA per email, sent from the founder, real signature with
phone number. Personalization slot quality > volume.

### 1A. Agency sequence (4-touch) — priority segment 1 (D-004)

**Touch 1 (day 0)** — Subject: `weird media for {client or category} briefs`

>
> When a client brief calls for "something people will actually talk about," this is an
> answer you can buy off a menu. Worth 20 minutes to see the catalog and pricing?

**Touch 2 (day 3)** — Subject: `re: weird media for {client or category} briefs`
> One concrete example: sponsoring a real Denver player at a $200-entry poker tournament —
> logo placement + short-form content, $450–$1,500 depending on tier, venue-approved,
> disclosure-compliant, proof delivered in 48h. That's a line item, not a production.
> Would that ever fit a {client_type} brief?

**Touch 3 (day 7)** — Subject: `the proof part`
> The reason I think agencies will care: every campaign ships with a proof package —
> timestamped photos, 1080p+ video (horizontal + vertical), disclosure evidence — before
> the seller is paid. You get client-ready evidence, not screenshots of promises.
> 20 minutes this week or next?

**Touch 4 (day 14)** — Subject: `closing the loop`
> Haven't heard back, so I'll stop here. If unusual-but-safe real-world placements are ever
> useful for a pitch, my catalog and prices are a reply away. Good luck with {something
> real and specific}.

## 5. Interview scripts (research, not sales — say so up front)

### 5A. Buyer interview (~12 questions, ~25 min; do not lead the witness)

Open: "I'm researching how {agencies/brands} buy unusual real-world marketing. I'm building
something in the space, but this call is me learning, not pitching. OK to take notes?"

1. Walk me through the last campaign you ran that wasn't standard digital ads. What was it?
2. How did you find and book it? What sucked about that process?
3. Who decided, and who signed off on the money?
4. What did it cost, all-in? What would it have been worth at 2x the price? (listen; don't anchor)
5. When you hear "sponsor a real person or a real-world moment," what comes to mind?
   What's the first risk you think of?
6. What proof or reporting do you need to call something like that a success internally?
7. What's a "brand-safety no" for you — categories, contexts, people?
8. What budget bucket would this come from, and how big is that bucket per quarter?
9. What lead time do you need between idea and live?
10. Have you bought influencer content? How did the ROI conversation go afterward?
11. If a menu of vetted, proof-backed real-world sponsorships existed for Denver, what would
    have to be on it for you to look twice? (open catalog question — take verbatim notes)
12. Ask price LAST and indirectly: "For {the thing they liked in Q11}, what price would make
    it a no-brainer, and what price would make it a joke?" (Van Westendorp-lite, unanchored)

Close: "May I follow up when I have a live catalog?" → tags them for outbound honestly.

### 5B. Seller interview (~10 questions, ~20 min)

1. Tell me about {their activity — the poker games, the dog walking, the commute, the event}.
   How often, where, who sees it?
2. Have you ever been paid to feature a brand? How did it come about, and for how much?
3. What would make wearing/carrying/displaying a brand feel fine vs. gross to you?
4. Any brands or categories you'd refuse? (log — feeds brand-safety matching)
5. If a sponsorship paid ${X}, what would you happily do for it? What's clearly not worth it?
6. Comfort with taking photos/video of yourself to spec? Any equipment?
7. We'd handle sales, payment, and rules; you'd get 80% of the package price after proof is
   accepted. Straight reaction? (tests A-03 without defending the number)
8. Reliability check: what could realistically make you cancel or no-show? (schedule, weather, day job)
9. What venues/organizers would need to say yes for your listing to work? Do you know them?
10. Who else do you know who has a sponsorable moment like yours? (supply referral engine)

## 6. Discovery-call script with qualification (30 min)

1. **Frame (2 min):** "Goal today: figure out if there's a fit worth proposing. If not, I'll
   say so and point you somewhere better."
2. **Their world (8 min):** current marketing mix, what's working, what "talked-about" would
   be worth to them.
3. **Concept fit (8 min):** walk 2–3 catalog items near their brand; watch reactions.
4. **Qualification (8 min) — must leave with all four:**
   - **Budget:** "Packages run $450–$1,500; managed campaigns start at $1,500 and most land
     $1,500–$5,000. Which range is realistic this quarter?" (< $250 = disqualify per D-005;
     be kind, keep for nurture.)
   - **Timeline:** target live date; < 10 business days triggers the D-006 +20% rush fee —
     say so now, not on the invoice.
   - **Decision maker:** "If you love the proposal, who else has to?" Get name + role.
   - **Brand-safety constraints:** prohibited categories/contexts/competitors; disclosure
     requirements on their side; legal review needs.
5. **Close (4 min):** "I'll send a proposal within 48 hours with 2–3 options. Payment terms:
   packages are paid at booking; managed campaigns are 50% to schedule, 50% at proof
   acceptance. Any surprise there?" Book the decision call before hanging up.

## 7. Campaign-proposal template (structure)

1. **Their goal, restated in one sentence** (their words from discovery)
2. **Options (2–3),** each: name · concept in 2 lines · concrete deliverables & proof spec ·
   dates · price (package price + 5% buyer service fee shown as a line item, per D-005; or
   managed quote with pass-throughs itemized + 15% coordination markup, per D-006)
3. **What we don't promise:** no guaranteed impressions; audience figures carry evidence or
   say "no estimate provided" (D-020/D-008 constraint language)
4. **How it works:** venue approval → execution → proof in 48h → 5-day review → done
5. **Terms:** payment terms (§10), cancellation, one revision round, disclosure requirement
6. **Expiry:** proposal valid 7 days (dates/inventory are real constraints, not fake urgency)
7. **Next step:** one sentence, one link/button

## 8. Follow-up sequence rules

- Proposal sent → follow up day +2 (check questions), day +5 (decision nudge + expiry
  reminder), day +8 (close-out: "I'll assume timing's wrong — inventory goes back on the
  shelf. Say the word to revive.")
- Every follow-up adds something (an answer, a photo, a relevant example) — never "just
  bumping this."
- Verbal yes → payment link same hour. Verbals decay ~daily (hypothesis; track it).
- No-decision after close-out → nurture list: case-study sends only, monthly max.
- All touches logged with next_action + next_action_date; no contact leaves a call or email
  thread without one.

## 10. Closing & payment terms (recorded rule)

- Close on the decision call: "Which option do you want to run first?" Then silence.
- **Package bookings: 100% payment upfront at booking.** Nothing is reserved on a promise.
- **Managed campaigns: 50% to schedule, 50% invoiced at proof acceptance.** This is the
  standard; do not negotiate structure, only scope.
- Buyer pays package + 5% service fee (D-005); $250 transaction minimum; managed minimum
  $1,500 (D-006); rush +20% under 10 business days.
- Payment via invoice/payment link at the moment of verbal yes — same hour.
- Refund posture per doc 07 §10: fast, generous, founder-decided. We defend trust, not deposits.
- Never call held funds "escrow" (D-013 language rule); say "payout releases after you accept proof."
