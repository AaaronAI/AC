# 02 · Category Definition — "Verified Sponsorable Moments"

**Date:** 2026-08-28 · Conforms to D-003 (category), D-016 (trust & safety is
launch-blocking), D-020 (honesty constraints). All examples below are illustrative
inventory concepts, not live listings; audience claims in real listings must carry an
evidence field or say "no estimate provided."

---

## 1. Definition

A **verified sponsorable moment** is a unit of real-world attention — a person, activity,
object, space, event, or stunt — packaged for a brand to sponsor at a fixed price, that is:

1. **Lawful** — no legal violation for seller, buyer, or platform; regulated categories
   (gambling-adjacent, alcohol, cannabis) only with explicit structuring and review.
2. **Consensual** — every identifiable participant has consented; no ambush marketing of
   unwilling third parties.
3. **Safe** — no unreasonable physical risk to seller, public, or property; risky
   concepts require mitigation (and insurance where applicable) before approval.
4. **Venue-approved** — where the moment occurs on someone else's property, written
   venue approval is obtained **before** branding is confirmed (first-class listing
   field, D-016).
5. **Packagable** — defined deliverables, dates, price, and restrictions; bookable as a
   unit at ≥ $250 (D-005 minimum).
6. **Provable** — completion can be evidenced with timestamped photo/video/GPS/receipts;
   payout releases only after proof acceptance (D-013, D-016).

All six are gates, not scores. Fail one → not sponsorable on this platform.

## 2. What does NOT qualify

- Anything unlawful, or lawful-but-prohibited by policy (`13-trust-safety-and-compliance.md`):
  weapons, adult content, political campaigning, medical claims, harassment or targeting
  of individuals, danger stunts without mitigation, anything involving minors.
- **Undisclosed sponsorship.** Every campaign carries clear paid-partnership disclosure
  (FTC endorsement guides; A-14). "Stealth" placements are rejected at moderation.
- **Gambling stakes.** A sponsor may pay for placement/content around a poker tournament
  (D-008) but may never hold a financial interest in the gambling outcome (A-15; counsel
  review pending).
- **Unverifiable audience promises.** Listings may not guarantee impressions or reach
  (D-008 constraint). Audience figures require evidence or the label "no estimate provided."
- **Moments the seller doesn't control** — e.g. "I'll get your logo on the stadium
  jumbotron" without a venue agreement.
- Inventory below the $250 package minimum (D-005) — not worth the trust-layer overhead.

## 3. Package anatomy

Every listing is built from the same six blocks. This is the product.

| Block | Contents | Example (poker Tier 2, D-008) |
|---|---|---|
| **Placement** | What is branded, where, when, how long; venue-approval status | Logo patch on shirt + table-side sign *where venue permits*, one tournament day |
| **Deliverables** | Enumerated, countable outputs | Placement + 5 photos + 1 short vertical video + 1 recap post with disclosure |
| **Proof** | Evidence standard and delivery window | Timestamped photos/video, entry receipt, geotag; delivered ≤72h post-event |
| **Rights** | What the buyer may do with content, for how long | Organic social reuse 12 months; paid usage = upsell (doc 04 of business model) |
| **Exclusivity** | Category/competitor lockout scope and duration | Single sponsor for the event day; no category exclusivity beyond it unless bought |
| **Restrictions** | Legal/venue/safety constraints, disclosure requirements, what's NOT promised | No impression guarantees, no share of winnings, venue may limit placement zones, #ad disclosure mandatory |

## 4. Taxonomy — 14 inventory types with Denver-realistic examples

Examples are concepts we believe are feasible in Denver; none is validated until a venue
or seller confirms (A-05, A-06).

| # | Type | Definition | Denver-realistic examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Person** | An individual's presence/identity carries the brand for a defined period | Branded gear on a Wash Park run-club regular for a month; a barista's branded apron + counter card at an amenable indie café (venue approval required) |
| 2 | **Activity** | A recurring practice someone already does | A climber's 20 gym sessions in branded chalk-bag/tee (gym approval); a dog walker's daily Cheesman Park route with branded leash + weekly content |
| 3 | **Moment** | A one-time life event with natural attention | A marathon runner's Colfax Marathon finish-line moment; a graduation-party photo backdrop; a (fully consenting) proposal picnic setup |
| 4 | **Object** | A physical item branded and placed in view | A food-truck's napkin dispensers at Civic Center Eats; branded cornhole set at a brewery patio (venue approval); a busker's guitar case wrap |
| 5 | **Outfit** | Clothing as placement | Branded ski jacket worn all season at A-Basin (resort rules checked first); bartender crew tees at a consenting LoDo bar for a weekend |
| 6 | **Vehicle** | Cars, bikes, trailers as moving placement | Rideshare driver's doorwrap for 30 days; a food trailer wrap at Sloan's Lake events; cargo-bike courier panniers downtown |
| 7 | **Space** | A controlled physical area | Coffee-shop window vinyl for a month; coworking-lobby product shelf; a garage-sale "presented by" takeover in Berkeley neighborhood |
| 8 | **Event** | A gathering with an organizer able to grant rights | A 60-person trivia night title sponsorship; a neighborhood 5k water station; a climbing-gym comp side-event |
| 9 | **Challenge** | A public attempt at a defined feat | "Summit 5 fourteeners in one summer, branded" content series; 30-day cold-plunge challenge at a consenting facility |
| 10 | **Trip** | A journey producing content across locations | A branded van-life weekend circuit (Denver→Moab) with daily content; a brewery-tour crawl across 8 Denver breweries (each venue consenting) |
| 11 | **Performance** | A live artistic act | A 16th Street Mall busker set with branded backdrop (permit required); an open-mic comedy night "presented by"; a mural painted live at a First Friday art walk |
| 12 | **Content concept** | A repeatable content format anchored in the real world | "Rating every Denver green chile" series with brand integration; "Denver dog of the week" park shoots |
| 13 | **Brand activation** | A produced mini-experience we operate (managed, D-006) | Free-coffee cart outside a tech office (permits/property consent); branded photo-op at a farmers market booth we rent |
| 14 | **Public stunt** | A lawful, permitted attention-grabbing act | The D-008 poker-tournament sponsorship (flagship); "100 branded bike commutes" (D-017); a giant branded snowman build in a permitted park (D-017-adjacent) |

Types 13–14 are managed-only at launch; 1–12 can be seller-listed but are
concierge-reviewed (D-001, D-016). Venue-dependent examples all carry the approval gate.

## 5. The Sponsorship Value framework

A transparency framework, **not a guaranteed-ROI score**. It exists so buyers can compare
unlike inventory on the same axes and so we price consistently. We display factor
ratings and their evidence; we never sum them into a single "value score," and we never
translate them into promised outcomes (which would violate D-008/D-020).

| Factor | What it captures | How it's evidenced |
|---|---|---|
| **Uniqueness** | How novel/memorable the moment is; PR/story potential | Moderator judgment + comparable listings |
| **Audience fit** | Who is plausibly present/watching and whether that matches the buyer | Seller-provided description; audience numbers only with evidence, else "no estimate provided" |
| **Location** | Foot traffic, neighborhood relevance, venue quality | Address/venue facts; public traffic data where it exists |
| **Duration** | Exposure window (hours ↔ season) | Package terms |
| **Content output** | Count/type/quality of usable assets delivered | Enumerated deliverables; seller's prior content samples |
| **Exclusivity** | Whether the buyer owns the moment or shares it | Package terms |
| **Seller reliability** | Track record of on-time, on-spec completion | Platform history (completions, disputes); "new seller" is an honest label |
| **Verifiability** | How strong the proof can be | Proof plan (timestamped media, receipts, GPS, third-party attestation) |
| **Measurement** | What, if anything, can be counted (attendees, riders, scans) | Only mechanically countable things (QR scans, promo codes); everything else is explicitly unmeasured |
| **Operational complexity** | Concierge hours, production, coordination burden | Ops estimate; drives pricing and managed-vs-self-serve routing |
| **Legal/venue restrictions** | Permits, venue rules, regulated-category constraints | Restriction block of the package; approval status shown |

Uses of the framework:
- **Moderation:** low verifiability or high legal complexity routes to human review or rejection.
- **Pricing guidance:** high uniqueness + high content output supports the upper band;
  low duration + no exclusivity anchors near the $250–$450 floor.
- **Buyer UI:** factors are displayed with evidence, so a $450 and a $1,500 package are
  comparable at a glance.
- **Explicit non-use:** it is never presented as a prediction of business results.

## 6. Category boundaries in one sentence each

- **vs. influencer post:** we sell the real-world moment plus the content; the post alone
  is not our category.
- **vs. OOH:** our inventory is human and episodic, not fixed structures.
- **vs. experiential agency:** we productize small activations at fixed prices; bespoke
  $50k builds are not our category (until much later).
- **vs. gig work:** the buyer sponsors a moment that exists (or is credibly staged),
  not labor on demand; the seller's authentic context is the asset.

   (A-05/A-06 — the first 25–50 curated listings will tell us; expect to kill 2–4 types.)
2. Does "audience fit" need a structured schema (age/interest tags) at launch, or is
   free-text + evidence enough for concierge matching? Lean: free-text until self-serve.
3. Where is the line between type 12 (content concept) and plain influencer content?
   Working rule: a real-world placement or event must be intrinsic, not backdrop — needs
   sharper policy language before self-serve.
4. Insurance triggers: which factor combinations (safety risk × public space) mandate an
   event rider vs. our general policy? Needs the insurance broker conversation (doc 21
