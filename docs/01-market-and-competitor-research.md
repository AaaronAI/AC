# 01 · Market & Competitor Research

**Date:** 2026-08-28 · **Method:** live web research (URLs cited per claim) + reasoning
from comparables. FACT = verified against a cited source on the date above. REPORTED =
third-party claim we could not confirm on the vendor's own site. HYPOTHESIS = our
reasoning, unvalidated. Per D-020, no fabricated market sizes appear anywhere in this doc.

## 1. Competitor and substitute landscape

Nobody does what we do; many do adjacent things. The scan below covers the categories a
buyer would consider instead of us.

### 1.1 Influencer marketplaces

| Platform | What it does | Pricing / take (verified where possible) | What it doesn't cover |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Collabstr** | Self-serve marketplace to hire creators for posts/UGC | FACT: brands pay 10% marketplace fee on Free/Pro, 5% on Premium; creators pay 15% on payout; Pro $299/mo, Premium $399/mo ([collabstr.com/pricing](https://collabstr.com/pricing), [coldiq.com](https://coldiq.com/tools/collabstr)) | No real-world placement, no venue/permit handling, no physical proof-of-activation |
| **#paid** | Managed-ish creator marketplace, standardized pricing, usage rights | FACT: plans from $499/mo ("Just Content"), Grow $699/mo, Advanced $999/mo ([getapp.com](https://www.getapp.com/marketing-software/a/hashtag-paid/pricing/), [hashtagpaid.com](https://hashtagpaid.com/)) | Content only; the "moment" itself isn't the product |
| **Aspire** | Enterprise influencer CRM/SaaS | REPORTED: ~$2,000/mo, annual contract, quote-only ([stackinfluence.com](https://stackinfluence.com/blog/influencer-marketing-platform-pricing)) | Tooling, not inventory; enterprise-only price point |
| **Grin** | Influencer management SaaS | FACT (published plans): usage-based tiers $0–$1,500/mo ([stackscored.com](https://www.stackscored.com/pricing/influencer-marketing/grin/)); REPORTED older quotes ~$2,500/mo annual | Same — software, no supply, no fulfillment |

**What we learn:** (a) take rates of 15–25% combined are accepted marketplace norms;
(b) $299–$999/mo SaaS is a proven agency price point — relevant to our deferred D-007
subscriptions; (c) standardized packages + upfront pricing (Collabstr, #paid) is what made
creator buying self-serve. We copy the packaging pattern, not the inventory.

### 1.2 OOH / ad marketplaces

| Platform | What it does | Pricing (verified) | Gap vs. us |
|---|---|---|---|
| **AdQuick** | Marketplace for 420k+ US billboards/OOH across major media owners | FACT: billboards ~$250–$30,000+/mo depending on format/market, CPMs ~$2–$18; marketplace campaigns have a **$25,000 minimum spend** ([adquick.com/billboard-cost](https://www.adquick.com/billboard-cost), [adquick.com/marketplace](https://www.adquick.com/marketplace)) | Fixed physical inventory only; $25k floor excludes our entire buyer segment |
| **Blip** | Self-serve pay-per-play digital billboards | FACT: from $0.01 per 8-second "blip," daily budgets from ~$20, no minimums or contracts ([blipbillboards.com/pricing](https://www.blipbillboards.com/pricing/), [blipbillboards.com/cost](https://www.blipbillboards.com/cost/)) | Screens only; no humans, no moments, no content output |

**What we learn:** Blip proves micro-budget real-world advertising demand exists at the
long tail (tiny businesses buying $20/day billboards). AdQuick proves the packaging/
transparency play works on physical inventory — and its $25k floor is a visible wall we
price under.

### 1.3 Sponsorship platforms

| Platform | What it does | Pricing (verified) | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| **SponsorUnited** | Sponsorship intelligence/data (2.2M+ deals tracked) for pro sports & entertainment | FACT: quote-based premium plans, no published pricing ([sponsorunited.com](https://www.sponsorunited.com/), [saasworthy.com](https://www.saasworthy.com/product/sponsorunited)) | Data about big deals; doesn't transact, ignores the long tail |
| **OpenSponsorship** | Marketplace connecting brands with 25k+ athletes | FACT: brand plans Full-Service $2,000/mo, ELITE $5,000/mo; commission charged on cash deals (rate not published); micro-athlete campaigns typically $500–$5,000 ([opensponsorship.com/pricing/brand](https://opensponsorship.com/pricing/brand), [opensponsorship.com blog](https://opensponsorship.com/blog/how-much-does-athlete-sponsorship-cost)) | Athletes only; mostly social deliverables; no ops/proof layer for physical activation |
| **SponsorMyEvent** | Marketplace matching event organizers with sponsors | FACT: free to list, **15% commission** on closed deals ([sponsormyevent.com](https://sponsormyevent.com/), [youreventkit.com](https://youreventkit.com/tools/sponsormyevent/)) | Events only; matchmaking, not managed fulfillment; no proof standard |

**What we learn:** the $500–$5,000 sponsorship order value is real and already transacted
(OpenSponsorship micro-athletes) — direct support for A-01's price band. A 15% success
commission (SponsorMyEvent) is below our 25% combined take, but they do no fulfillment,
verification, or proof — our extra 10 points must visibly pay for that.

### 1.4 Gig / talent platforms

| Platform | Model | Take (verified) | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fiverr** | Gig marketplace | FACT: 20% seller commission + 5.5% buyer fee (+$2.50 under $50) ([freelancenation.com](https://freelancenation.com/blog/fiverr-fees-explained), [hireinsouth.com](https://www.hireinsouth.com/post/fiverr-pricing)) | Digital services; no physical-world trust/permits layer |
| **Cameo** | Personalized celebrity video | FACT/REPORTED: ~25% platform take, talent keeps ~75% ([sacra.com/c/cameo](https://sacra.com/c/cameo/), [forbes.com](https://www.forbes.com/sites/mnunez/2020/01/06/30-under-30-how-cameo-makes-tens-of-millions-from-selfie-videos-and-d-list-celebrities/)) | Video-only product; no brand-activation format |

**What we learn:** our D-005 structure (20% seller-side + 5% buyer fee) is almost exactly
Fiverr's, i.e. pre-validated as a fee shape sellers and buyers already accept at these
order values. Cameo proves ordinary consumers and brands will pay individuals for
one-off personal moments at 25% take.

### 1.5 Experiential agencies and local ad networks

- **Experiential agencies** — REPORTED (agency-published guides, treat as marketing but
  directionally consistent across several): small brand activations commonly start around
  $10,000, single-day pop-ups ~$15,000–$30,000, typical focused activations
  $25,000–$50,000+ ([altterrain.com](https://altterrain.com/how-much-does-experiential-marketing-cost/),
  [barnastics.com](https://www.barnastics.com/news/experiential-marketing-activation-cost-2026),
  [actionflipbooks.com](https://actionflipbooks.com/blog/brand-activation-cost-guide/)).
  Gap: bespoke, slow, high-minimum. We are the productized bottom of their market.
- **Local ad networks / event booths** — FACT: 10x10 booths at craft fairs/festivals run
  ~$150–$500, larger festivals $200–$800+ ([vendorsmap.com](https://vendorsmap.com/blog/how-to-set-booth-fees));
  farmers-market stalls $10–$75/day. Gap: the brand does all the work; no content, no
  proof, no staffing. This is the DIY substitute at our exact price point — our package
  must beat "we could just buy a booth" on effort and content output.

## 2. Where SponsorThis sits

**The uncovered gap (FACT by absence):** across the 12+ platforms above, none sells
**long-tail, lawful, real-world inventory** — people, objects, vehicles, moments, stunts —
as **fixed-price packages with venue approval, rights, and timestamped proof**, at
$450–$5,000. Influencer platforms stop at the screen. OOH platforms stop at fixed
structures. Sponsorship platforms stop at athletes and big events. Agencies start at
$10k+. The quadrant (real-world × long-tail × sub-$5k × verified) is empty.

Our defensibility is not the marketplace software; it's the **operational trust layer**
(moderation, venue approval, permits, disclosure compliance, proof standard — D-016) that
makes weird inventory safely buyable. That layer is expensive to copy and is exactly what
the adjacent players have avoided building.

## 3. Substitute analysis — what buyers do today

| Substitute | Typical cost | Strength | Weakness we exploit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Paid social (Meta/TikTok) | ~$5–$15 CPM (HYPOTHESIS band; varies widely) | Measurable, scalable | Saturated, skippable, zero story value; rising CAC (unverified but widely reported) |
| Micro-influencer post | FACT: ~$100–$500 typical for IG micro feed post; reels higher ([influencermarketinghub.com](https://influencermarketinghub.com/influencer-rates/micro-influencer-rates/), [influee.co](https://influee.co/blog/instagram-influencer-pricing)) | Cheap, easy | Content-only; no physical presence; ROI equally soft |
| Event booth / local sponsorship | FACT: $150–$800+ per booth (above) | Physical presence | Self-service burden: staffing, design, no content, no proof |
| Experiential agency | REPORTED: $10k–$50k+ entry | Full service, high production | Price floor excludes SMB/startup; slow |
| Do nothing / DIY stunt | ~$0 cash | Free | Founder time, permits, legal risk, no distribution |

**Willingness-to-pay reasoning (HYPOTHESIS, built on the FACTs above):** a brand already
paying ~$150–$500 for one micro-influencer post — soft ROI, content-only — is being asked
to pay $450–$1,500 for a package containing (a) a real-world branded placement, (b) 2–10
pieces of usable content, (c) timestamped proof, (d) disclosure-compliant rights. On a
per-content-asset basis alone the package can price at or below influencer rates, with the
activation "free." At the top of our band, $5,000 buys a managed micro-activation that an
agency would quote at $15k–$30k. The claim we must validate (A-01) is not "will brands pay
for attention" — they demonstrably do at both bounding price points — but "will they pay
*us*, for *this* inventory, at these prices." That is what the 20 buyer interviews and
30-day pilot exist to answer. No amount of comparable-shopping substitutes for a signed PO.

## 4. Market-size framing — Denver bottom-up sketch

Per D-020 we do not quote a TAM. Below is a **serviceable-market sketch for Denver only**,
labeled estimate, with methodology. It answers one question: *is Denver big enough to
support the D-010 goal and 12-month base scenario?* — not "how big is the category."

**Inputs (each labeled):**

- FACT: ~378,000 small businesses in the Denver metro (JPMorgan Chase Institute small
  business snapshot, 2022 data: [jpmorganchase.com](https://www.jpmorganchase.com/institute/all-topics/business-growth-and-entrepreneurship/denver-small-business-snapshot)).
- FACT/REPORTED: ~325 marketing/advertising agencies tracked in the Denver area (Clutch
  research, 2021: [clutch.co/agencies/denver](https://clutch.co/agencies/denver)).
- REPORTED (single blog source, low confidence): 450+ annual festivals/events in the
  Denver metro ([303happenings.com](https://www.303happenings.com/blog/denver-festivals)).

**Methodology (every filter is an assumption, stated):**

| Step | Filter | Value | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Denver-metro businesses | ~378,000 | FACT above |
| 2 | × share that buys *any* paid marketing beyond a website | 20% → ~75,000 | HYPOTHESIS (conservative; no source found) |
| 3 | × share plausibly interested in real-world/experiential at $450–$5k | 3% → ~2,300 | HYPOTHESIS; anchored on experiential being a minority tactic |
| 4 | + agencies (each a multi-campaign buyer) | +325 | FACT/REPORTED above |
| 5 | × reachable & closable by one founder in year 1 | 2% of (3)+(4) → **~50 buying accounts** | HYPOTHESIS from founder-led outbound math (A-11) |
| 6 | × campaigns/buyer-year (mix of 1× and repeat) | ~1.5–2.5 → **~75–125 campaigns/yr** | HYPOTHESIS; repeat rate is untested (A-02, A-10) |
| 7 | × blended AOV $1,200–$2,000 | **~$90k–$250k serviceable GMV/yr** | D-005/D-006 price bands |

**Reading:** the sketch brackets our 12-month scenarios (conservative 75 campaigns /
$110k GMV; base 141 / $223k; upside 272 / $445k — see doc 21). Conservative and base fit
inside the sketch; **the upside scenario requires beating step 5's reach assumption**
(more than ~50 accounts, i.e. a repeatable non-founder sales motion or inbound from PR) —
which is honest: upside is an execution claim, not a market-size claim. Denver is big
enough to pass or fail D-010 on the merits; market size is not the binding constraint in
year 1. Willingness to pay is.

**What would change this analysis:** interview data showing step 3 is materially above or
below 3%; actual close rates from the first 100 outbound contacts (A-11); festival/venue
receptivity (A-06).

## 5. Implications for strategy

1. **Price under the agency floor, above the booth fee.** $450–$5,000 sits in white space
   between DIY ($150–$800) and agencies ($10k+). Hold the band (D-005/D-006).
2. **Sell content output as the ROI anchor.** Comparables show buyers accept soft ROI
   when they get assets (influencer market). Every package ships content (doc 02).
3. **Our 25% combined take matches Fiverr and Cameo, exceeds SponsorMyEvent's 15%.**
   The delta is justified only if verification + fulfillment is visible in the product.
   If sellers balk (A-03 falsifier: >40% decline), revisit — but comparables say they won't.
4. **Watch AdQuick/Blip, not influencer platforms**, as the likely future entrants into
   packaged real-world long-tail inventory; they already have OOH buyer relationships.
5. **Deferred SaaS (D-007) has strong comparables** ($299–$999/mo across Collabstr Pro,
   #paid, OpenSponsorship) — good future margin layer, correctly deferred until repeat
   buyers exist.
